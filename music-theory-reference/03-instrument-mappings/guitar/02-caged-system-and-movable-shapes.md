# 02 CAGED System and Movable Shapes

The **CAGED system** is a fretboard framework built around five open chord shapes: **C, A, G, E, D**.

This module is guitar-specific. It assumes the fretboard coordinates and standard tuning from `01-fretboard-layout-and-standard-tuning`.

## 1. What CAGED Is

CAGED is a way of organizing the fretboard by five familiar chord shapes that:

- Can be played as open chords near the nut
- Can be **moved** up the neck as barre or partial-barre shapes
- Connect to one another across the fretboard in a repeating order

The name is the order those shapes are typically traversed:

$$ C \rightarrow A \rightarrow G \rightarrow E \rightarrow D $$

and then the sequence repeats: after D comes C again at the next octave region.

Each shape is a *fretboard region* with a characteristic root position, string set, and finger pattern.

## 2. The Five Shapes

The CAGED shapes are not just chords; they are **maps** of where the root and other chord tones live in a region of the neck. For each shape:

- The **root** notes are at particular places on certain strings.
- Once you know the root locations, you can move the whole shape to any key.
- The shape also gives you associated scales, triads, arpeggios, and voice-leading connections.

In all descriptions below, think of the root as the note that names the chord.

### C shape

- Root on the 5th string and also on the 2nd string in the open C family.
- In its familiar open form, the shape is built around the 5th-string root and uses the 4th, 3rd, and 2nd strings in a characteristic triad layout.
- As a movable shape, it is typically barre’d at the top with the index finger acting like a movable nut.

### A shape

- Root on the 5th string.
- The A shape is especially easy to barre and is one of the most broadly used movable shapes.
- It offers a strong, compact hand frame and a clear layout of roots on one string.

### G shape

- Root on the 6th string and also a root on the 3rd string in the open G family.
- The G shape is often wider and less compact than E or A, and it connects outward to both the low and high strings in important ways.
- In movable use, it often involves partial barring and a wider stretch.

### E shape

- Root on the 6th string.
- The E shape is one of the most important barre shapes; it is extremely compact and widely used for both chords and scales.
- Many fretboard approaches start with E as a primary movable shape.

### D shape

- Root on the 4th string.
- The D shape is compact and highest-register among the five; it often connects upward toward higher voicings and string sets.

## 3. Why the Order Matters

The CAGED sequence is not alphabetical by convenience. The order reflects how shapes **connect** across the neck:

- Each shape typically leads into the next shape one or more frets higher.
- The sequence defines a logical path up the fretboard through overlapping regions.
- Because the five shapes each cover different root-string layouts and voicings, they give multiple ways to play the same chord in different regions.

In other words, CAGED is less “five isolated shapes” and more “five interlocking fretboard regions.”

## 4. Moving a Shape to a New Key

To move any CAGED shape to a new root:

1. Locate the root on the string(s) the shape uses.
2. Place the shape so that its root lands on that note.
3. Keep the same finger pattern relative to the new root position.

For a barre shape, the index finger commonly becomes a movable “nut,” allowing the same shape to slide along a string.

For example, using the **E shape** with a 6th-string root:

- If the 6th string at a fret is your desired root, build the E shape from there.
- That shape then represents the major chord of that root in that region.

The exact fret number is irrelevant to the *shape*; only the root location matters.

## 5. Root Locations and String Families

CAGED shapes are characterized partly by which string(s) carry the root in their primary version:

| Shape | Primary root string(s) in common form |
|-------|----------------------------------------|
| C     | 5th string (and often 2nd string)     |
| A     | 5th string                             |
| G     | 6th string (and often 3rd string)     |
| E     | 6th string                             |
| D     | 4th string                             |

This is one reason the system is useful: it gives you chord representations on different strings, so you can choose a voicing that fits the musical context.

## 6. CAGED as a Fretboard Map, Not Just Chords

A key insight is that each CAGED shape contains more than the full chord:

- **Triads**: the essential 1–3–5 is embedded in the shape.
- **Arpeggios**: chord tones are laid out in an order you can traverse.
- **Scales**: each shape is strongly associated with a scale region and fingering around it.
- **Voice-leading paths**: the connections between shapes suggest smooth transitions.

In practice, CAGED learners often use the shape as a gateway to the notes, scales, and patterns in that region, not just to a single chord voicing.

## 7. ASCII Representation of the Five Shapes (Conceptual)

A conceptual “shape card” notation uses a compact ASCII grid where:

- Vertical lines represent strings (high to low or low to high, depending on the diagram convention)
- Horizontal fret axis is marked for the region in question
- `●` or `×` marks fretted notes, open strings, or roots

For example, a conceptual movable **E shape** with roots on the 6th string can be shown schematically as:

```
      root
       ●
E str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
B str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
G str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
D str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
A str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
E str ───●────────────────────────────────────────────────
      fret n
```

Each `●` here stands for a fretted tone at the indicated position. The exact strings and frets depend on the shape version; this is a schematic, not a literal position diagram.

A schematic **A shape** with a 5th-string root might read:

```
E str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
B str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
G str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
D str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
A str ───●────────────────────────────────────────────────
E str ─────────────────────────────●────────────────────
      fret n
```

A more compact **D shape** with a 4th-string root:

```
E str ───●──────────────────────────
B str ───●──────────────────────────
G str ───●──────────────────────────
D str ───●──────────────────────────
A str ──────────────────────────────
E str ──────────────────────────────
      fret n
```

These are intentionally schematic. They show that each shape is defined by a root placement and a characteristic pattern relative to it.

## 8. Connecting the Shapes in Practice

A common CAGED exercise:

- Start with a C shape at a given root.
- Find the same root in the adjacent A shape area.
- Continue to G, E, D, then to the next C region.
- Observe how the same chord name appears in multiple voicings on the way up.

The connections show two things:

- Any chord has multiple “addresses” on the neck.
- The fretboard is not just one shape mapped in one place; it is a set of overlapping regions.

## 9. CAGED and the G/B Major Third

Because standard tuning has a major third between the 3rd and 2nd strings, the CAGED shapes do not slide around the neck in a perfectly uniform way on all string pairs. On the G/B boundary, shapes shift in a characteristic way; that shift is part of what makes each shape distinct and useful in its own region.

When learning CAGED, pay special attention to how each shape crosses that boundary — the pattern of fingerings and root locations changes there.

## 10. Using CAGED for Scale and Arpeggio Work

CAGED shapes are often used as containers for:

- Major and minor scales in the shape’s region
- Arpeggios that follow the chord tones of the shape
- Target-note practice for improvisation

This makes CAGED not only a chord-voicing system but also a way to tie together harmony, scale, and fretboard navigation in one framework.

## 11. Summary

- CAGED organizes the fretboard around five shapes: C, A, G, E, D.
- Each shape is a movable region with characteristic root positions and finger patterns.
- The order reflects how shapes connect across the neck, not just alphabetical convenience.
- CAGED gives multiple voicings for the same chord in different registers and string families.
- The G/B major third makes the shapes behave differently across that boundary.
- Treating CAGED as a fretboard map — not just five chords — is what makes it powerful.
